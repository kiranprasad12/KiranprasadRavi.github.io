Fixture design with reinforcement learning

At FBK I rebuilt an existing German codebase in English and extended it. It is a Python environment where a PPO agent places locators, clamps and point contacts on a workpiece mesh.

A finite element model (Pynite) checks how much the part moves under load. The goal is a maximum displacement below 0.2 mm.

Training runs on the university HPC cluster. The system is still at an early training stage, and I am improving it step by step.

Python, PPO, Stable-Baselines3, Gymnasium, Pynite, Gmsh, SLURM

An illustration of the idea, not real training results.

Thermal imaging of a 3D printer

In a digital twins course, our group watched a 3D printer at work with an MLX90640 thermal sensor. The sensor sends a 32 by 24 pixel temperature image over I2C to an ESP32. We compared different print speeds and wall sizes. I did the wiring myself.

About me

I did my B.E. in Mechanical Engineering at Anna University in Chennai, and I am now doing my Master's in Commercial Vehicle Technology at RPTU in Kaiserslautern. I did an internship at Brakes India Private Limited, and I work as a student assistant at the Chair of Manufacturing Engineering and Operations Management (FBK) at RPTU.

At FBK I build a reinforcement learning system for automated fixture design. It combines Python, finite element simulation and a high-performance computing cluster. This is where I built up my Python and machine learning skills.

What interests me most is the point where mechanical engineering meets software: simulation, control strategies, sensors and data. My courses include camera systems and 2D image processing for driver assistance, and digital twins.
